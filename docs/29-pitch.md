# 29 — Pitch

> **Implementation update — 27 September 2026:** ERgency turns a confirmed
> arrival account into both a clinician-reviewed brief and a source-linked
> suggested review order. The list is a place to start, not a locked queue:
> every report remains openable and the clinician owns every clinical decision.

## One sentence

ERgency helps a clinician decide which report to open first by keeping the
recorded account, what is unclear, and the source words behind the suggestion
visible.

## The story

1. **ERgency — AI-suggested review order. Clinician decides.** An emergency
   arrival starts as a messy account, not a clean record.
2. **FIFO can hide what needs a second look.** New reports may contain
   uncertainty, contradictions, or important account cues while the list keeps
   growing.
3. **New report → information gaps and account cues → suggested review order.**
   Information gaps are deliberately weighted more heavily than account cues.
4. **Every report stays openable.** The displayed order gives a clinician a
   place to start; it does not prevent them opening any report in any order.
5. **Demo the proof.** Submit fictional information, show the new report enter
   the list, open it to show the exact source quotes behind the suggestion, and
   then open a lower-listed report to demonstrate unrestricted access.
6. **Close.** ERgency suggests where to start; the clinician owns priority,
   next step, and approval.

## What the judge will see

The submitted fictional account is stored exactly as confirmed. The optional
model can only return source quotes: information gaps such as uncertainty or
conflict, plus account cues. The application converts those counts into a
written suggested-review band and an initial list order. After opening the
report, the clinician can read the supporting quotes and make their own
priority and next-step decision.

## What we will not claim

No diagnosis, treatment recommendation, ATS category, patient safety
conclusion, wait-time reduction, ROI, device certification, or real-patient
validation. “Suggested last” is not reassurance; it only describes the
starting display order for a fictional demonstration.

## User, buyer, and next proof

The user is the triage clinician; the potential buyer is a health system. The
next validation question is whether clinicians find the source-linked starting
order useful enough to use at the next fictional arrival—and when they choose a
different report instead.
