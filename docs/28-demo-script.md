# 28 — Demo script

> **Implementation update — 27 September 2026:** Submit a fictional tablet
> account, show that the active list has a suggested starting order, then open
> both the surfaced report and a lower-listed report. The list never blocks
> access; clinicians alone record priority, next step, and approval.

Five minutes. Use the hosted application with a clinician already signed in on
one device and the fictional tablet check-in ready on another. The written form
is the rehearsed fallback if microphone capture is unavailable.

## 0:00–0:30 — Opening

> ERgency is an AI-suggested clinician review order. It helps staff decide
> where to start reading—not who is sicker, what care is needed, or what the
> final priority should be.

## 0:30–1:20 — Capture a fictional account

Complete a short fictional voice or written check-in, correct the confirmed
text if needed, and submit it.

> The captured account is write-once. The system can only point back to its
> words; it cannot rewrite them into a diagnosis.

## 1:20–2:10 — Suggested order

Switch to `/queue` and refresh if required.

> A new report has been added to the shared list. ERgency uses source-linked
> information gaps and account cues to suggest where a clinician might start.
> Information gaps weigh more heavily than account cues. Every report remains
> visible and selectable.

Point to the numbered order marker and written suggested-review label. Do not
call it an urgency colour, a triage category, or a patient priority.

## 2:10–3:20 — Why it was surfaced

Open the surfaced fictional report.

> Here is why it was surfaced: these are exact words from the submitted
> account. The system has shown uncertainty or an account cue, not a diagnosis
> or an instruction. This is a suggested review order, not a clinical decision.

Read one quoted source line. Do not infer a condition from it.

## 3:20–4:05 — Prove the list is not locked

Return to the queue and open a lower-listed report.

> The order is a place to start, not a gate. A clinician can inspect any report
> at any time, in any order.

## 4:05–4:35 — Clinician decision

On a draft report, point to the blank decision controls.

> ERgency has not selected a priority or next step. The clinician records both
> before approval, and the approved handover becomes read-only.

## 4:35–5:00 — Close

> The user is the triage clinician; the potential buyer is a health system.
> ERgency suggests where to start, while the clinician owns every clinical
> decision. This is a fictional-data hackathon demonstration, and the next
> proof is whether clinicians find this source-linked order useful.

## If the suggestion is unavailable

> The report was still captured and remains open to review. This list falls
> back to chronological order rather than hiding or blocking any report.
